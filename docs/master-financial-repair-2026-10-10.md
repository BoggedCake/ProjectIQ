## 4. Development profitability

The existing feasibility engine retains supported, conditional preliminary and exploratory eligibility gates. Unsupported/prohibited or failed-standard proposals are not made achievable to produce financial results. Contributions that remain unknown retain their assessment gate.

Gross realisation minus total economic development cost yields estimated profit. Margin on revenue = profit / gross realisation; return on cost = profit / total economic cost. Retained land opportunity cost belongs in economic feasibility, not a new cash payment. Incremental cash expenditure is economic total less retained-site value; existing mortgage principal is not an expense. Delivered cost components, professional/authority allowances, contingency, finance, selling expenses and provisional net tax assumptions reconcile through the ledger. Net GST/tax treatment is not professionally verified; income-tax or margin-scheme advice is not certified.

Confirmed household/compare debt is reusable rather than requested again. Entered zero remains debt-free, while unknown debt remains unknown. Development accommodation enters economic total/profit once; refundable bond is separate cash only. Explicit development holding allowances take precedence; entered monthly holding expenses replace the default allowance.

Residual remains a retained-land scenario. Acquisition-specific residual/finance/tax inputs remain future work.

## 5. Shared residential finance and holding model

The existing commercial engine exports residentialFinance; no competing module or hosting was introduced. Current renovation, extension, add-level and development UI pathways reuse the framework with their existing project costs.

Existing and new rates, remaining terms, repayment types and IO periods are separate. New and replacement borrowing default to P&I independently of an existing IO loan. The repayment headline now uses the first occupied monthly schedule, including IO expiry, paid-off debt and construction conversion. A future IO reversion repayment is disclosed.

Mortgage top-up/split draws immediately. Construction borrows through weighted progressive mid-month draws, pays interest on drawn balances during construction and amortises after completion/delay. Monthly rows audit balances, drawdowns, interest and repayments. Household interest is paid monthly; the existing specialised development schedule retains capitalised interest where modelled.

Cash savings, reserve, offset withdrawal and user-confirmed redraw are separate. Redraw adds debt; offset withdrawal reduces the interest benefit. Cash-funded projects do not invent new borrowing interest. Fees and existing-loan refinancing are explicit. Net incremental finance compares against the original mortgage baseline; a negative net result denotes refinancing savings, not a negative lender charge. Comparison accepts that generated result with model provenance, while negative entered cost allowances remain invalid.

Monthly rates, insurance, utilities and other holding costs are editable and included once. Existing mortgage principal/repayments are cash flow, not another construction cost. During-works cash requirements use actual existing/new repayments less the counterfactual original mortgage repayments, including newly repaid principal; economic finance and project costs continue to use interest only. A zero-rate 120,000 top-up over ten years needs 12,000 repayment cash during twelve-month works despite zero interest. Gross project cash requirement is 132,000 while economic project expenditure remains 120,000. Offset withdrawal can raise interest cost without increasing fixed P&I payment cash. Rent/storage/moving are separate expenditure; bond is refundable cash outside economic cost. Delay adds relevant holding/rent/storage. Weekly rent is supported by the engine using 52/12, with explicit monthly rent taking precedence; consumer entry currently uses monthly rent.
